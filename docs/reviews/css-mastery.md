# Review: Modern CSS Mastery (`css-mastery`)

## Verdict

A strong, coherent course. The Waypoint Conf schedule page really does run from the first lesson to the last. Each lesson teaches one mechanism from the browser's side, and the exercises check computed behaviour (resizing slots, flipping `dir`, toggling `data-theme`, spying on `startViewTransition`) rather than exact text, so any correct solution passes. The Arabic is natural MSA, sounds like a working developer wrote it, and uses its terms consistently (the RTL lesson even adds a localised sentence for Arabic readers). The cascade, specificity and `@layer` explanations, the container-query rules, `:has()`, the fluid-type maths (I recomputed every slope and intercept) and the RTL/logical-properties lesson were all correct. The problems I found were a few mechanism errors around invalid-at-computed-value-time (the most serious: the relative-color lesson recommended a static fallback that `var()` defeats, which is the exact trap the course teaches earlier), one assessment item with two correct answers, an easing claim that didn't match its numbers, and support statements that had drifted. I checked every Baseline date against the current `web-features` dataset (status as of October 2026). `content-visibility: auto` is 2025, not 2024. Light-dark images, Firefox's missing `display` animation and the "widely available" milestones reached in 2025–2026 were missing. I verified the mechanism claims in headless Chromium. All 56 further-reading URLs resolve. The validator and the exercise checker are both clean.

## Verified and left as is

- Cascade order, `!important` reversal across origins and layers, unlayered-beats-layered, `revert-layer`, `@import … layer()` placement rules.
- Specificity of `:is()`/`:not()`/`:has()` (most specific argument), `:where()` zero weight, forgiving lists only in `:is()`/`:where()`, nesting `&` as `:is()`.
- `@property` behaviour (unset on a syntax mismatch, inherited fallback to the initial value, typed computed values). I re-ran the 1-4 exercise logic in Chromium.
- Subgrid with a single span: all children overlap in one track (checked in Chromium).
- Flexbox/grid arithmetic in all quizzes and exercises (auto-fit/auto-fill counts, `fr` with gaps, clamp() results, fluid-type slopes and intercepts).
- Baseline dates I confirmed: cascade layers 2022; container size queries Feb 2023; container style queries May 2026 (Firefox 151); `@property` July 2024 (Firefox 128); nesting and `:has()` Dec 2023; subgrid Sept 2023; `oklch()`/`color-mix()` 2023; relative color 2024; `light-dark()` colors 2024; `@starting-style`/`transition-behavior` 2024; same-document view transitions Oct 2025 (Firefox 144); cross-document view transitions and scroll-driven animations not Baseline (no Firefox); `@scope` March 2026. Scroll-driven animations are an Interop 2026 focus area (confirmed).

## Could not fully verify

- "`style(display: grid)` (style queries on regular properties) isn't supported anywhere yet" (l-css-2-5): I found no shipping implementation, but I can't prove the negative. The wording ("yet") is already hedged.
- Whether every engine respects `scroll-padding` for focus-navigation scrolling (l-css-3-5). Chromium and Firefox do. I didn't test Safari.
